Since 1976

Fifty years
at altitude

Named for Ethiopia's highest peak, Dashen Terara has never been about speed — just doing one thing right, for a very long time.

Since 1976, we've served homemade berbere and slow-cooked classics in a stone-walled garden villa in Hayahulet, with live music filling the courtyard every night.

Only the pure in heart make

The music never stops at Dashen Terara

From the kitchen

Recipes passed down, not printed on paper

Homemade spice blends, slow-cooked stews, and a full coffee ceremony to close every meal — everything is made in-house, the same way it was in 1976.

Doro wot

Chicken simmered for hours in a deep berbere sauce, finished with a boiled egg and our own clarified spiced butter.

Signature

Kitfo

Hand-minced beef warmed gently in mitmita and niter kibbeh, served the traditional way with ayib and gomen.

House favorite

Shiro

A slow-simmered chickpea sauce, smooth and peppery, ladled generously over fresh injera.

Vegetarian

Special tibs

Lamb or beef seared with garlic, rosemary and jalapeño, brought sizzling straight from the pan.

Grilled

Beyaynetu

A full vegetarian platter of lentils, cabbage, beets and greens — a fasting-day favorite, served any day.

Coffee ceremony

Green beans roasted, ground and brewed at your table over incense — the closing ritual of every meal here.

The Hayahulet compound

A villa, a garden, and a live band that never quite stops

Old trees, open lawn and a stone villa surround a courtyard built for gathering. As evening falls, a live cultural ensemble — kirar, kebero and traditional dance — fills the grounds under open sky.

  • Stone villa architecture, indoor and courtyard seating
  • Live cultural music and dance, nightly
  • Tej and areke bar, plus full coffee ceremony
